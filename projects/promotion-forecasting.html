Promotion forecasting & profitability

I developed a forecasting and reporting workflow to explore how promotional price scenarios could influence demand and commercial outcomes. The project connected statistical modeling in R with financial views in Power BI.

The business question

What happens after a price changes?

A promotion can attract customers and increase volume, but lower selling prices and promotional costs also affect the commercial picture. I built a way to compare scenarios across demand and financial metrics.

Forecasting approach

Model customer response across scenarios

I used R to prepare historical observations and build separate regression estimates for units, page views and buyers. The workflow applied model outputs to a range of hypothetical selling prices and organized the forecasts for reporting.

Demand

Estimate units at different scenario prices to explore how volume may change.

Customer activity

Use separate forecasts for page views and buyers to add context around demand.

Sales value

Combine estimated units and scenario price to calculate projected gross merchandise value.

Projected GMV = units × price
How to interpret the forecasts

The estimates describe patterns in historical data under the model’s assumptions. They are inputs for scenario planning; actual promotion performance can differ as customer behavior, campaign execution and market conditions change.

Technical workflow

From historical observations to decision support

R forecasting

I prepared the modeling data, fitted price-based regressions, generated scenario-level predictions and structured outputs for reporting.

Business interpretation

I connected demand estimates with a financial perspective so decision-makers could consider volume, sales value and profitability together.

The approach is best used with business context and validation, particularly when historical data is noisy or a proposed scenario falls outside the price range observed in the data.
