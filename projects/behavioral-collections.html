Behavioral Collections & Delinquency Prediction

A behavioral credit-risk classification workflow built to estimate the probability of reaching 30 days past due (DPD30), validate discrimination across monthly cohorts, and support risk-based customer prioritization.

Problem & approach

From transaction history to earlier risk signals.

Business problem

Binary delinquency outcomes alone cannot convey how the risk ranking performs across future cohorts. The aim was to develop behavioral risk predictions and assess whether scores meaningfully distinguish customers who later reach DPD30.

Modeling approach

Behavioral signals derived from observed cash flow, repayment history, customer attributes and loan characteristics feed supervised classification. Separate inference and evaluation routines apply a documented probability threshold and track discrimination, capture, calibration-related scores and score stability.

Interpretation

What these findings show

Risk discrimination across cohorts

The model achieved recorded ROC-AUC values above 0.79 in each of the seven evaluated months. June had the highest ROC-AUC (0.872621), while March was lowest (0.790281).

Concentration within the highest-risk decile

Lift ranged from 2.222 to 4.422 across cohorts. The reported top 10% of scores captured between 22.2% and 44.2% of observed DPD30 positives, depending on the cohort.

Important qualification — population shift

June PSI was 0.363727, materially above the other six months (all below 0.12). Stronger ROC-AUC in June does not eliminate concerns about distribution drift; investigating cohort composition and monitoring stability remains necessary. The supplied outputs do not establish a fully deployed production outcome or a causal business uplift.
