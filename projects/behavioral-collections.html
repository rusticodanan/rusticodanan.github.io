Problem & approach

From transaction history to earlier risk signals.

Business problem

Binary delinquency outcomes alone cannot convey how the risk ranking performs across future cohorts. The aim was to develop behavioral risk predictions and assess whether scores meaningfully distinguish customers who later reach DPD30.

Modeling approach

Behavioral signals derived from observed cash flow, repayment history, customer attributes and loan characteristics feed supervised classification. Separate inference and evaluation routines apply a documented probability threshold and track discrimination, capture, calibration-related scores and score stability.

Analytical pipeline

Development and validation workflow

01

Observation-aware feature engineering

Constructed salary activity, variability, cash-flow trends, account-balance behavior, historical repayment indicators, and loan characteristics using time-dependent feature windows.

02

Classification experimentation

Evaluated classification approaches, including Random Forest, LightGBM and other challenger algorithms, with parameter tuning and class-imbalance considerations in the development notebook.

03

Risk scoring & thresholding

Scored observations using a saved model bundle and applied a 0.16 classification threshold in the documented monthly inference workflow.

04

Out-of-time monitoring

Evaluated January–July 2026 cohorts in the later inference workflow with ROC-AUC, Gini, KS, precision, recall, F1, F2, Brier score, top-decile capture, lift and PSI.

Salary disruption was highly differentiating

The EDA slide defines a salary dip as an MOB0 salary credit at or below 80% of the median over the previous six months. The observed first-due DPD30 rate was approximately 39.7% with the dip versus 8.5% without it.

Existing underwriting deciles were less separating

Among borrowers at the delinquency stage examined in the presentation, the existing decile groups exhibited relatively similar subsequent DPD30 rates (approximately 18.2%–23.2%). This supported the case for new post-booking behavioral signals.

Lower observed income activity

Borrowers reaching DPD30 generally had lower salary credits at MOB0. The EDA noted zero salary credits as a possible indication of employment or income disruption.

Employment history matters

Shorter company tenure, higher employer attrition, and shorter time since a first loan were associated with increased deterioration risk in the presentation's descriptive analysis.

Interpretation of key signals

Income shocks

Salary-dip indicator and MOB0 salary credit magnitude dominate the selected feature importance results, consistent with the EDA.

Employment stability

Company tenure and current or prior employer attrition contribute additional context about income continuity.

Borrowing history

Days since first and last loan offer behavioral history beyond static demographic segmentation.

Borrower segments

FTB status and marital-status encodings add information, but must not be interpreted as causal drivers or standalone treatment rules.

Reported model-score contrasts

In the presentation's SHAP-based summary, the reported predicted-risk contrast for salary dip was 4.55×, FTB versus RB 1.84×, and single versus non-single 1.44×. These are descriptive model-output contrasts, not causal estimates.

Careful interpretation

Because the outcome and the business process are related to income and employment stability, these signals help explain predictions. However, model explanations are not proof that changing any one attribute would change an individual's outcome.

Interpretation

What these findings show

Risk discrimination across cohorts

The model achieved recorded ROC-AUC values above 0.79 in each of the seven evaluated months. June had the highest ROC-AUC (0.872621), while March was lowest (0.790281). However, June’s eligible borrower population changed following an FTB company-code data fix, so its result is not directly comparable with prior months without accounting for this shift.

Concentration within the highest-risk decile

Lift ranged from 2.222 to 4.422 across cohorts. The reported top 10% of scores captured between 22.2% and 44.2% of observed DPD30 positives, depending on the cohort.

June 2026 — explained population shift following an eligibility data fix

June was not a like-for-like cohort. According to the project’s operational context, the Data Team corrected the handling of missing company codes for first-time borrowers (FTBs). The fix made previously excluded borrowers eligible, increasing the population of eligible loans and changing the mix of borrowers entering the collections model.

This change provides an important operational explanation for June’s unusually high Population Stability Index (PSI = 0.363727), compared with below 0.12 in the other six evaluated months. June also recorded the highest ROC-AUC (0.872621) and top-decile lift (4.422×). These figures should be interpreted in the context of a changed eligible population, not simply as a sudden improvement in model quality. The timing and mechanism of the fix are supplied as operational context; the saved performance metrics alone do not independently establish the causal contribution of the fix.
