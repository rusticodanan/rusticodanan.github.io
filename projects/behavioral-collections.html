Behavioral Collections & Delinquency Prediction

I developed a behavioral credit-risk modeling workflow to identify borrowers at risk of reaching 30+ days past due across any due date. The final June 2026 study extended the earlier first-due-only model to support risk-informed collections prioritization throughout the early delinquency lifecycle.

Business challenge

Move from bucket-only treatment to behavior-informed priorities

Earlier version

Estimated 30+ DPD at the borrower's first due date. It was a narrower early repayment risk use case.

Final expanded version

Estimates deterioration risk across successive due-date cycles, using more recent salary, liquidity, employer and payment-timing signals. It is designed to support prioritization, not automate collections decisions.

Business objective: help Collections concentrate limited outreach capacity on early-delinquency accounts with higher modeled probability of reaching 30+ DPD. Realized treatment or recovery benefits were not established by the model-validation results alone.

Payroll continuity signals

Salary amounts and the ratio of due-month salary credits to historical median salary showed meaningful differences between borrowers who rolled to 30+ DPD and those who cured. Lower salary continuity corresponded to elevated observed risk.

Employer stability and tenure

Employer attrition, shorter company tenure, and changes in financial activity were associated with repayment deterioration, offering additional information beyond static demographic attributes.

Existing credit deciles

The legacy underwriting deciles were less discriminating within the already-delinquent population: the analysis of the six existing groups showed about 30%–37% reaching DPD30, motivating post-booking behavioral scoring.

Salary and due-date interaction

Risk varied with the timing of repayment obligations. Borrowers with salary continuity around end-of-month dates generally behaved differently from those facing payroll disruptions; this interaction appeared across the training and initial OOT cohorts.

Aggregate figures and findings from the June 4 presentation, EDA slides 12–14 and appendix slides 16–17. These describe observed associations, not causal effects.

Salary dip: model-risk contrast

The presentation reports approximately 5.53× higher predicted rollover probability in the salary-dip subgroup relative to the comparison group. This is a model-derived subgroup contrast, not an intervention effect.

Repayment timing matters

The due-month salary ratio and whether the due date occurs at month-end interact with payroll stability. A calendar feature may be important because of when income becomes available—not because the calendar date itself causes delinquency.

Operational interpretation

A threshold of 0.16 expands coverage to identify more eventual deteriorations, trading off false-positive workload against missed-risk accounts.

Note on precision and recall: 71% recall means 71% of actual 30+ DPD cases in OOT1 were flagged; 53% precision means 53% of flagged observations became 30+ DPD in that cohort.

The slide deck presents the model as a decision-support tool for prioritizing outreach, not as evidence that it was deployed or that collections recoveries improved.

Monitoring & governance

Watch for operationally driven population shifts

The June 4 final model documented stable initial OOT1–OOT3 discrimination (AUC roughly 0.79–0.80) and PSI from 0.02 to 0.11. Model performance should also be checked after data-quality or eligibility changes that alter which loans enter the scored portfolio.

Later June 2026 operational context (separate from the June 4 deck): a fix to missing company codes for first-time borrowers expanded eligibility and changed the loan population. That later observation may explain part of the June population shift seen in subsequent monitoring. Do not compare the earlier deck's OOT figures with a later monthly inference run as though they are identical model snapshots.

Watch discrimination and calibration

Track ROC-AUC/Gini, precision, recall, top-decile lift and actual event rates by segment and over time.

Investigate drift before retraining

Use PSI and feature-level monitoring to understand the root cause of shifts—model deterioration, changing borrower mix, data pipeline corrections or policy changes—before deciding to retrain.
