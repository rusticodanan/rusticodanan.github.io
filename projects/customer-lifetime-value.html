Customer Lifetime Value & Experiment Profitability

I developed a financial decision-support calculator to bring Net Margin (NM) and Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) into the evaluation of lending experiments. The goal was to move beyond the profitability of a single booking and assess how different strategies could affect customer value across subsequent loans.

Business problem

Immediate margin doesn't tell the whole story.

Net Margin

Measures the economics of a loan. It provides the near-term profitability view needed when comparing changes in pricing, tenor, loan amount or other lending strategies.

Customer Lifetime Value

Extends the profitability view across future borrowing opportunities, accounting for the likelihood that a customer returns and for the expected margin of each subsequent loan.

The decision to enable: compare experiments on both their effect on the current loan and their expected effect on the customer relationship over time.
Model methodology

From first loan to lifetime contribution.

01 / Define cohort

Start with an FTB risk segment and establish the original borrower population.

02 / Reconstruct journey

Follow loan sequence from FTB through RB1, RB2 and later repeat borrowing.

03 / Profile loan mix

Distribute subsequent loans by current risk decile, tenor and average loan amount.

04 / Assign margin

Use NM lookups to estimate per-loan contribution under corresponding loan economics.

05 / Weight future value

Apply the probabilities of reaching later loan stages and accumulate expected contribution.

CLV = NM₁ + P(RB1) × E[NM₂] + P(RB2) × E[NM₃] + …Here P(RB2) means the cumulative probability of reaching the second repeat loan, not the conditional rate alone. The workbook models expected contribution by loan sequence.

Borrowing continuation

I used loan-sequence counts to derive continuation rates, then expressed the probability of reaching each later stage relative to the original FTB population.

Changing customer economics

Repeat borrowing can change loan size, tenor and risk decile. Rather than carrying the initial loan's margin forward unchanged, the framework incorporates the observed mix of these characteristics at each stage.

Experiment evaluation

Two lenses for better lending decisions.

The calculator provides a foundation for structuring evaluations of lending experiments that may influence pricing, loan terms, borrower conversion, and future borrowing behavior.

Key takeaways

A framework for the next decision.

Look beyond booking

A strategy can improve first-loan economics without necessarily improving lifetime customer value.

Reflect lifecycle changes

Probability of repeat borrowing and changing risk and tenor mix should influence customer profitability estimates.

Make experiments comparable

Using NM and CLV together establishes a more complete basis for evaluating future lending strategies.
