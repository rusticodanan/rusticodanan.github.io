Behind every loan is a story

I developed a CLARA segmentation framework to understand who our borrowers are, how their needs differ, and how those differences relate to repayment risk.

The challenge

One borrower population, different needs

First-time and repeat borrowers bring different financial histories to a loan. I built separate segmentation models to make those differences visible and support more relevant customer strategies.

Cluster 1

Young Single Credit Builders

Early-career borrowers with mostly small loans. Many are building experience with credit while managing bills and essentials.

Opportunity: Pair responsible credit-building messages with gradual progression linked to repayment.

Cluster 2

Married Stability Managers

66% aged 36 or older · 100% married

The stories behind repeat-borrower risk

Mark · Young Single Credit Builder

Mark represents borrowers who are early in their careers and building a credit record while managing everyday expenses. This segment shows the earliest signs of repayment stress: its 5.48% MOB2 rate is close to the 5.96% early-warning indicator and is the highest of the three RB groups. Smaller financial buffers may make it harder for some borrowers to recover from an unexpected expense. This makes early engagement and manageable credit progression especially relevant.

Nanay Vilma · Married Stability Manager

Nanay Vilma represents borrowers who plan around household bills, tuition and family needs. Her segment shows some early stress, but its 4.23% MOB2 and 8.28% MOB6 rates are lower than Mark’s and below their respective indicators. The pattern is consistent with a more established household-planning profile; stable employment and shared household resources may help some customers absorb financial pressure.

Ramon · Experienced High-Usage Optimizer

Ramon represents experienced borrowers who use credit more often to manage different needs. Even with higher loan usage, his segment has the lowest observed rates at both MOB2 (3.07%) and MOB6 (5.98%). The combination of longer employment tenure and borrowing experience offers a plausible explanation to explore: frequent credit use can coexist with strong repayment performance.

What this adds to risk management

Segmentation offers a view of risk alongside a borrower's profile and usage. These observed group differences can guide monitoring and further investigation; they do not establish causation or replace individual credit assessment.

The stories behind first-time borrower risk

Anthony · Established NCR Breadwinner

Anthony represents an established borrower who plans credit around household expenses. His segment shows the strongest repayment profile of the six groups: 4.65% at MOB2 and 9.27% at MOB6, both below the early-warning indicators. Longer employment tenure and a more established financial routine are consistent with this resilience, although the analysis does not establish what caused it.

Jenny · Young Income Stabilizers, clusters 2–5

Jenny represents an early-career borrower using smaller loans while building income stability. These regional groups generally face more repayment pressure than the established segment. Their paths differ over time: cluster 2 rises from 5.78% at MOB2 to 14.00% at MOB6, while cluster 3 records 6.06% and 12.29%. This suggests monitoring beyond onboarding as well as early support.

Jenny · Luzon provinces, cluster 4

Among young income stabilizers in Luzon provinces, the rates are 7.31% at MOB2 and 15.26% at MOB6, both above their indicators. The pattern suggests repayment pressure is visible early and remains present later, making both early contact and continued monitoring relevant.

Jenny · NCR, cluster 5

The NCR group shows the highest rate at both checkpoints: 8.51% at MOB2 and 17.39% at MOB6. The pattern points to immediate and sustained repayment stress. For a young borrower managing essential expenses in NCR, early intervention and clear repayment support may be especially useful; the data alone cannot establish the reason for the difference.

Roberto · Family Anchor, cluster 6

Roberto represents older, largely married borrowers managing family obligations. His segment starts below the MOB2 indicator at 5.69% and reaches 12.32% at MOB6, still below the 13.43% indicator. The later checkpoint invites a closer look at sustained pressure over the loan term: unexpected household needs could make recovery harder for some families, a hypothesis to test rather than an observed cause.

One framework, two applications

Marketing teams can use the profiles to tailor engagement. Risk teams can compare segment performance at different points in the loan lifecycle and investigate where monitoring or early support may be useful.

Methodology

Building interpretable segments at scale

01 · Prepare the data

I brought together loan, application and savings data. Ordinal fields were converted to numeric values and scaled, while nominal fields were one-hot encoded. Repeat-borrower inputs included loan history.

02 · Fit and compare

I ran CLARA across candidate cluster counts from 3 to 6. CLARA draws samples, finds representative medoids, and assigns observations to the nearest representative.

03 · Refine the features

I reviewed silhouette scores as features were removed, balancing separation with business interpretability. The repeat-borrower score increased from 0.19 to 0.53, and the first-time score from 0.29 to 0.44.

04 · Assign new borrowers

I developed R workflows to prepare borrower features and assign records to the closest saved medoid using Manhattan distance, processing records in chunks.

Silhouette measures how closely observations fit their own cluster compared with other clusters; it is not a prediction accuracy metric.

Outcome

A shared view of the borrower

The framework connects customer profiling with observed portfolio performance. It gives teams a common starting point for discussing customer needs, retention opportunities and differences in delinquency.
