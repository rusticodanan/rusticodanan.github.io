Behavioral Collections & Delinquency Prediction

I developed a behavioral credit-risk modeling workflow to identify borrowers at risk of reaching 30+ days past due across any due date. I subsequently extended the earlier first-due-only model to support risk-informed collections prioritization throughout the early delinquency lifecycle.

I expanded the original first-due model into a due-date-level behavioral risk framework, incorporating payroll, liquidity and employer signals to predict which accounts were likely to progress to 30+ DPD.

Business challenge

Move from bucket-only treatment to behavior-informed priorities

Earlier version

Estimated 30+ DPD at the borrower's first due date. It was a narrower early repayment risk use case.

Final expanded version

Estimates deterioration risk across successive due-date cycles, using more recent salary, liquidity, employer and payment-timing signals. The expanded scoring approach allows risk to be reassessed as new repayment and cash-flow information becomes available.

Business objective: rank early-delinquency accounts by expected deterioration risk so Collections can prioritize outreach, allocate capacity more effectively and adapt treatment thresholds to operational needs.

Payroll continuity signals

Salary amounts and the ratio of due-month salary credits to historical median salary showed meaningful differences between borrowers who rolled to 30+ DPD and those who cured. Lower salary continuity corresponded to elevated observed risk.

Employer stability and tenure

Employer attrition, shorter company tenure, and changes in financial activity were associated with repayment deterioration, offering additional information beyond static demographic attributes.

Existing credit deciles

Among already-delinquent accounts, existing underwriting deciles showed limited separation: approximately 30%–37% progressed to DPD30 across six groups. This led me to emphasize recent borrower behavior rather than relying on pre-booking risk alone.

Salary and due-date interaction

Risk varied with the timing of repayment obligations. Borrowers with salary continuity around end-of-month dates generally behaved differently from those facing payroll disruptions; this interaction appeared across the training and initial OOT cohorts.

These EDA findings shaped the feature-engineering strategy: shifting from static borrower attributes to due-date-aligned behavioral and employment indicators.

Salary dip: model-risk contrast

Borrowers with a due-month salary dip had approximately 5.53× the modeled rollover risk of those without one. This reinforced the importance of payroll continuity in prioritizing accounts.

Repayment timing matters

The due-month salary ratio and whether the due date occurs at month-end interact with payroll stability. This suggests that repayment timing relative to payroll inflows is an important component of short-term liquidity pressure.

Operational interpretation

I selected a 0.16 threshold to identify more accounts likely to deteriorate, accepting a wider outreach population to reduce missed high-risk cases.

How to read these results: 71% recall means 71% of actual 30+ DPD cases in OOT1 were flagged; 53% precision means 53% of flagged observations became 30+ DPD in that cohort.

This scoring framework gives Collections a practical way to distinguish high-risk accounts within the same delinquency bucket and direct early outreach toward the accounts with the greatest modeled deterioration risk.

Monitoring & governance

Watch for operationally driven population shifts

Across January–March 2026, out-of-time AUC remained around 0.79–0.80, with PSI between 0.02 and 0.11. I also monitored population shifts alongside model metrics, particularly when upstream changes affected borrower eligibility.

June 2026 population shift: The Data Team corrected missing company codes for first-time borrowers, making previously excluded borrowers eligible and increasing the number of loans entering the portfolio. This changed the borrower mix and helped explain the significant PSI shift observed in June. I treated the change as an upstream data and eligibility event requiring closer monitoring of model stability and performance.

Watch discrimination and calibration

Track ROC-AUC/Gini, precision, recall, top-decile lift and actual event rates by segment and over time.

Investigate drift before retraining

I used PSI and feature-level monitoring to distinguish changing borrower composition and upstream data changes from genuine deterioration in model performance, informing when additional investigation or retraining would be appropriate.
